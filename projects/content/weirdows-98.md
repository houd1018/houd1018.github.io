:::overview
The traditional input system on a PC would be the keyboard and mouse, but have you ever thought about using the actual computer? In Weirdows '98, our team has put together an operational "fake computer" that players must interact with to progress through the game. We do this by using an Arduino hidden inside the computer case. (To clarify: the computer case is hollow and a hidden computer runs the game.)

- Built the gameplay: the fake OS in Unity
- Set up the location-based installation
:::

## How it plays

:::row
There are four controls on the fake computer: the power button, the floppy disk drive, a button on the side and a switch on the back.

The power button starts the boot-up scene. Players push the floppy disk in to access the documents on the PC. Then another error occurs: a motor inside the fake computer drags a small piece of metal, making a very loud sound, and the player has to hit the switch on the back to progress. Finally the computer "overheats" and a small gust of smoke comes out of the top, so the player hits the button on the side. (The smoke is made with a cotton swab, vegetable glycerin and a wire.)

In all, the experience gives non-tech-savvy players the excitement of playing with hardware and software. We give the computer itself life, along with the traditional escape-room fun of finding clues.
